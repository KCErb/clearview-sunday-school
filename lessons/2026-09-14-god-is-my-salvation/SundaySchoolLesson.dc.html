Why Isaiah is worth the effort
"The book of Isaiah contains numerous prophecies that ... have multiple fulfillments. One seems to involve the people of Isaiah's day ... Another meaning... refer[s] to events in the meridian of time... Still another meaning ... relate[s] to the events attending the Second Coming of the Savior. The fact that many of these prophecies can have multiple meanings underscores the importance of our seeking revelation from the Holy Ghost to help us interpret them."
President Dallin H. Oaks
"Scripture Reading and Revelation," Ensign, Jan. 1995

A quick timeline
c. 740 BC
Uzziah / Jotham. Isaiah's call occurs in the year Uzziah dies (Isaiah 6). Jotham is Ahaz's father.
c. 735 BC
Ahaz. Israel and Syria attack Judah. Ahaz appeals to Assyria. Isaiah 7–8.
732 BC
Assyria advances. Damascus falls; Assyria takes northern Israelite territory.
722/721 BC
Northern Israel falls. Samaria falls and the Northern Kingdom ceases to exist.
701 BC
Hezekiah. Assyria invades under Sennacherib, but Jerusalem survives (Isaiah 36–37).

Isaiah 1

In Isaiah 1, the Lord says of many religious ceremonies, rites, and holidays "I am full of", "I delight not in" and "my soul hateth". 

  • ●What did they fail to do that led to the Lord's disappointment (v17)?
  • ●Where do you see yourself in Isaiah's warning?
  • ●What can you do to ensure your religious devotion includes the justice and charity the Lord saw lacking?

Isaiah 2:4

In Isaiah 2:4, Isaiah prophesies that nations will beat their weapons that take life into tools that give it.

  • ●How might we apply this image in our own lives? What weapons might we need to beat into tools?
  • ●Have you seen friends or family exchange weapons of war to become peacemakers?
  • ●Who is someone you admire for their peacemaking skills?
Back to discussion
Isaiah 5:18

In Isaiah 5:18 we draw our sins and iniquities behind us like a cart being drawn with a rope.

  • ●What did Isaiah say we draw our cart with?
  • ●Are we dragging a burden, or holding on to something we treasure?
  • ●What makes it difficult to let go of something we know is holding us back?
  • ●How can you encourage others to let go of their cords and cart ropes?
Back to discussion
Isaiah 8:19–20

In Isaiah 8:19–20 we learn about the temptation to look to other sources — spiritualists, the dead — to determine what to do about our problems. Isaiah says to look to God and gives a test.

  • ●What was the test?
  • ●How does it apply in our day?
  • ●When has listening to divinely appointed sources helped you recognize truth and avoid being misled?
